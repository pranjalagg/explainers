How to read the machine

Type case and stick
The type case is the vocabulary: click a word to append it. The stick holds the context, at most eight lanes including the start marker.
Lane and tray
Each word runs down its own lane on a tray. The tray is the residual stream: three banks of numbers that steps add to and never overwrite. T says who I am, A says who came before me, O says what I will say next.
Portals with cards
Attention. Each lane keeps a key and a value in two cards per layer, one per head. Cords reach back to the lanes it reads, thick for a high weight. The cards stay put, which is the key-value cache.
Portals with drawers
Memories. A drawer opens when the tray matches its key and writes its votes for the next word into the tray.
Bridge, ruler and marble
The last lane is read out as scores, softmax makes them probabilities, and the ruler lays them out end to end. A marble dropped at a random point picks the word. The word returns by the rail to the end of the stick.

What is real and what is simplified

Every number in the figure comes from the arithmetic of a 2-layer, 2-head decoder with 26 numbers per lane: RMSNorm, before each sublayer, RoPE on queries and keys, SwiGLU memories (7 and 3 of them), causal masking and a residual stream. Nothing is faked.

What is simplified: the weights were written by hand from rules, not learned, so the three banks stay neatly apart. A trained model spreads the same information across all its numbers, with no labels, and uses many more heads, layers and memories. This toy only knows eight words, but the parts and the data flow are the ones in every modern language model.

For scale: GPT-3 has 96 layers, 96 heads and 12,288 numbers per lane, and reads 2,048 lanes at once. LLaMA 65B has 80 layers, 64 heads and 8,192 numbers per lane. Modern models also share key and value heads between groups of query heads to shrink the cache you see in the portal cards.
