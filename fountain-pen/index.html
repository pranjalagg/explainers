• Air channel lets a bubble in at 160 Pa
  • Ink channel pulls ink with 533 Pa
  • Paper pores (10 µm) pull with 8000 Pa

How it works

  1. The pin opens the cartridge. The feed ends in a hollow pin that pierces the cartridge's plug. It gives ink a way out and air a way in.
  2. Capillary action draws ink forward. Along the feed's ink channel the gap is only 0.15 mm, and a gap that small pulls liquid with about 533 Pa, enough to lift ink against gravity.
  3. The paper takes it from the nib. The slit carries ink to the tipping. Paper pores, about 10 µm across, pull with about 8000 Pa, far harder than the slit, so ink moves into the page as the nib drags over it.
  4. Air has to come back. Every microlitre of ink that leaves thins the air above the ink, so the pressure in the cartridge falls. At 160 Pa below the room a bubble forces in at the breather hole, runs back along the wider air channel and rises to the top of the cartridge. Seal that channel and the suction builds toward 533 Pa until the feed can no longer pull.
  5. The fins are a reserve and a catch basin. The channel delivers at most 0.35 µL/s, so a bold, fast stroke would starve; the fins hold about 6 µL to draw on and refill when you pause. They also soak up the ink that a warming cartridge pushes forward, instead of letting it blot the page.
  6. The nib flexes. Press and the two tines spread, the slit opens and the line gets wider, 0.4 mm at rest and up to 1.6 mm in this soft nib. A wider line asks for more ink, which loops back to step 5.

Try: press 12 weights at 55 mm/s and watch the fins drain, then ease off and watch them refill. Press Remove the fins and repeat. Press Warm the pen with the fins on and off.
